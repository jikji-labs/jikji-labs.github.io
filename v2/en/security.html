Design stage — before implementation

Enforce policy at execution boundaries

The design follows default deny, least privilege, subset scopes for delegation, and classification and inspection at input, tool and output boundaries.

Models and tools are outside the trust boundary

Model output is a request, not permission or approval. Validation proceeds through schema, authorization, budget, OS isolation, execution, output inspection and audit. Unsupported sandboxing must not silently become unrestricted execution.

PII and secrets

Classification and denied paths provide the first defense, followed by PII tokenization, minimal context and safe receipts. Raw prompts, tool outputs and secrets must not be retained indirectly in logs, knowledge or messages. Perfect detection of natural-language PII is not guaranteed.

Cancellation, failure and revocation

Policy-generation changes, expired leases, duplicate delivery and uncertain external writes need distinct handling. Detector and authorization failures deny execution; audit-write failures block new effects. Isolation, cross-tenant access, leakage and cancellation races still require implementation tests.
